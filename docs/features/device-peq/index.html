Hardware EQ

Hardware EQ (formerly Device PEQ) reads and writes EQ directly on supported audio hardware — DACs, dongles, streamers and some headphones — from the browser, with no extra software. Once written, the EQ lives in the device’s own memory and keeps working away from the computer.

It sits in the Equalizer panel, together with Link with uniPEQ, which reaches a device through the uniPEQ app instead. Hardware EQ holds one connection at a time: once you connect a device here or start a link, the other way in is hidden until you disconnect or unlink. For the workflow, see Equalizing audio.

Connecting

Section titled “Connecting”

Connect USB device is the way in for most dongles and DACs. The other connections sit right under it:

ConnectionBrowser APIExamples
USBWebHIDFiiO, Moondrop, Walkplay and KT Micro dongles, DAC/amps
USB serialWeb SerialJDS Labs, Nothing, FiiO, and Bluetooth serial ports
BluetoothWeb BluetoothSelect FiiO and Airoha-chipset devices
Network—IP-addressable devices such as WiiM streamers and Luxsin X9

The browser’s device picker only lists devices Hardware EQ knows how to drive. Once you pick one, it is looked up in eqcaps, an open database of what each EQ engine accepts — band count, filter types per band, frequency, Q and gain ranges with their step sizes, and the preamp — and how to talk to it.

  • Several devices fit. Some devices report the same identity (a Bluetooth serial port names no device at all). You’re asked which one it is. Where only the firmware tells them apart, the device is asked first, so you’re only asked if it doesn’t say.
  • Shared profile. Some devices are only known as a family that shares a chipset’s firmware. The panel says your exact model isn’t listed; the family’s limits still apply.
  • Not in the database. A device from a known maker is connected on that maker’s usual protocol, with limits taken from what the protocol can carry, and marked experimental. A device nothing can drive gets a Help add it link to the eqcaps inspector, which turns a connected device into a database entry.

Supported devices, under the connect buttons, opens the eqcaps catalog: every device and device family in the database, searchable and always current. Network devices aren’t in eqcaps; the Network connection lists them. The ? beside the connections says what each one is for.

The device’s limits

Section titled “The device’s limits”

While a device is connected, its limits are the active EQ constraint. Connecting never changes your bands: the ones the device can’t hold are marked, and Fit above the band list moves them onto the nearest values it accepts, as one undoable step. Fit never adds bands: if you have fewer than the device, the rest are written flat when you write to the device (see below). Hand edits and AutoEQ stay inside the limits from then on — a 5-band device gets a 5-band AutoEQ. Disconnecting restores whatever constraint you had picked before.

When there is something to know about the device’s profile — a shared or guessed profile, limits that haven’t been checked against a real device yet (with a Report wrong limits link), experimental support, a device that can’t be read back — an icon appears beside its name. A warning triangle means the limits themselves may be off; click it for the details.

Device EQ

Section titled “Device EQ”

Device EQ is what the device plays, and it changes the device as soon as you change it:

  • On a device with several presets, it’s a dropdown of those presets plus Off (EQ bypassed). Picking a preset switches the device to it, and Read and Write use that preset. If the device is on one of its own built-in presets, the dropdown says so; Write then saves to its first editable preset and switches to it. Presets the device lists as built-in can be played and read but not written: pick one of your own to write. On a device that reports its EQ was changed since a preset was loaded (a Qudelix after any edit), the dropdown shows Unsaved EQ, and Read and Write use the EQ the device plays without saving it to a preset. A change made in the device’s own app shows as Unsaved EQ only after Read on a directly connected device; linked through uniPEQ, it shows when uniPEQ reports the device’s status. With the EQ off, picking Unsaved EQ turns it back on as it was.
  • On a device that keeps a single set of bands (most dongles), it’s an on/off switch. Off bypasses the EQ inside the device; the bands stay saved on it.

Some devices can only read and write the preset they’re playing. While they’re on another one, or switched off, Read and Write are unavailable and the panel says why.

While a device is connected here, the audio player leaves the EQ to it, so what the device plays isn’t equalized twice.

Adding, renaming and deleting presets

Section titled “Adding, renaming and deleting presets”

A few devices keep their presets as a list that grows and shrinks, with names stored on the device: the Topping DX5 II and DX9 Discrete. For these, Device EQ lists the presets the device holds right now under its own names, and a Presets row under it has three buttons:

  • New adds an empty preset and switches the device to it, so Read and Write use it straight away. It’s unavailable once the device is full.
  • Rename renames the preset Device EQ shows. The name is stored on the device, which may only take a short one in plain letters and digits; a name it refuses is said in the dialog, and nothing is shortened to fit.
  • Delete removes the preset Device EQ shows, for good. The device may switch to another of its presets, and Device EQ follows it. The last preset can’t be deleted.

Rename and Delete are unavailable while Device EQ shows Off.

Devices with more than one EQ

Section titled “Devices with more than one EQ”

A few devices have two EQs: a Qudelix 5K has its user EQ and a separate headphone EQ (“SPK EQ”), each with its own bands, presets and on/off switch. For these, an EQ row appears at the top of the box, and everything else follows the one you pick: the limits on the band list, Device EQ, Read, Write and auto-write.

  • Picking another EQ reads nothing and leaves your bands as they are, like picking another preset (over the uniPEQ link, uniPEQ reads it into the band list instead). The limits change with it (the Qudelix 5K Plus headphone EQ has 20 bands, its user EQ 10), so bands that no longer fit are marked.
  • An EQ the device doesn’t have in its current mode is greyed out, and a line under the row says so. A Qudelix set to its single-EQ mode has no headphone EQ; its user EQ then has more bands, and the limits show the count the device reports.
  • If the device’s mode changes while it’s connected, the panel falls back to the main EQ the next time something fails for that reason, and says so.

Device settings

Section titled “Device settings”

Some devices have settings of their own beside the EQ, and the ones modernGraphTool can drive appear in the same box as Device EQ: a headroom, a crossfeed, a DAC filter, an output gain, and so on. Which ones depends on the device; most dongles have none.

  • A setting is applied as soon as you change it. It isn’t part of the band list: Write to device doesn’t send it, undo doesn’t revert it, and it has no effect on whether the device and the list match.
  • Each row shows what the device reports. If the device refuses a change, the row stays where it was and the status line says why.
  • Settings that belong to the EQ sit right under Device EQ, above a divider; the device’s general settings are below it. On a device with two EQs they are the picked EQ’s: each of a Qudelix’s EQs has its own headroom. A device can keep an EQ setting with each preset (a Qudelix keeps its crossfeed that way), so those rows are read again when you switch preset or read the device. Some devices refuse to change them while the EQ is off.
  • A setting that restarts the device asks first, and the panel reconnects by itself afterwards.
  • If a setting’s current value stops the device applying its EQ, the row and the status line say so.

Options keep the names the device’s own app uses.

Reading and writing

Section titled “Reading and writing”
  • Read from device loads the device’s EQ into the band list and turns the Equalizer on.
  • Write to device sends the band list. If the device can’t hold it exactly as shown — a gain past its range, a band its grid can’t place, more bands than it has — you first see what will change, band by band, and nothing is written until you confirm. Flat and disabled bands are written as 0 dB at their own frequency and Q, as the vendors’ apps do, so reading the device back shows the same layout. Device bands the list doesn’t fill are written flat too. On a device that can be read back, the preset is read first, so those bands keep the frequency and Q they already had.
  • One status line under the buttons says where things stand: what the last read or write did, and once you edit, that the list has changed since. A failed operation, a paused auto-write or a preset the buttons can’t reach takes that line over until it’s dealt with.

Writing automatically

Section titled “Writing automatically”

Turn on Write changes automatically and the device follows the band list: each change — a dragged band, an AutoEQ run, an undo, an import — is written a moment after you stop editing, even with the Equalizer panel closed. Turning it on writes the current list straight away; connecting a device with it already on writes nothing until you change something.

  • If a change would reach the device differently from what’s on screen — a value it can’t hold, a left- or right-only band on a device with one EQ for both ears — auto-write pauses and offers Review and write. It carries on by itself once the list fits again.
  • After switching Device EQ to another preset, your next change is written into that preset.
  • If a write fails, auto-write turns itself off until you switch it back on or connect again. The saved setting stays on, so one dropped connection doesn’t turn it off for later visits.
  • Devices that restart after every save don’t offer it.

Many devices store each write in flash memory, which wears with use. Auto-write sends one write per pause in editing, not one per movement, but it’s off by default for that reason.

Some devices restart to save what they’re sent. The panel waits a few seconds for the device and connects it again by itself. If it doesn’t come back, the panel offers Reconnect, which finds it again without the browser’s picker.

If a device is unplugged or goes out of range, it’s disconnected right away and your own constraint comes back; your bands stay as they are.

The preamp goes to devices that take one. A device that can’t lower its own level, or can’t lower it as far as the bands boost, gets a clipping warning.

Left and right

Section titled “Left and right”

Most devices have one set of bands for both ears. A write to one of those sends the L+R bands and tells you how many left- or right-only bands it skipped — see Per-channel EQ.

A few have a set per ear: the Qudelix headphone EQ (“SPK EQ”) and the Topping DX5 II. While one of those is the EQ in use, the Channel switch above the band list stays available, and:

  • Write sends each ear its own EQ: the L+R bands plus that ear’s own. A device with 20 bands per ear therefore takes 20 for the left and 20 for the right, with every L+R band counting once on each.
  • Read brings back both ears. Where they hold the same thing, the bands arrive as L+R bands; where they differ, a band both ears share stays L+R and the rest arrive as left-only and right-only bands.
  • The review before a write lists what changes by the band’s number in your list, whichever ear it belongs to. A change that reaches only one ear — an L+R band with no room left on one side, say — names that ear.

Linked through uniPEQ, one of these devices in uniPEQ works the same way, with a uniPEQ recent enough to carry both ears: see uniPEQ Link.

Network devices

Section titled “Network devices”

Pick Network, choose the device type, and enter the device’s address: an IP address (192.168.1.20, or an IPv6 address), or a .local name (wiim.local), with a :port if it needs one. Enter connects. Anything else is refused beside the field before a request is made: a web address with http:// or a path, a user name, spaces, and any public hostname such as example.com. That check is what stops a mistyped or pasted address from sending commands to another server. Private address ranges are not enforced, so a device reached over a VPN works.

Nothing is sent when you connect; the first request goes out when you press Read or Write. If the browser can’t reach the device, you get a message that says so instead of a generic failure.

  • HTTPS sites can’t reach HTTP devices. The Luxsin X9 only answers over plain HTTP, and a browser blocks that from a page served over HTTPS (mixed content). Use a deployment served over http:// on your own network, or run the tool locally. A WiiM answers over HTTPS with its own certificate, which the browser may refuse until you open the device’s address in a tab once and accept it.
  • Local Network Access. Chrome and Edge 145 and later ask before a public website connects to a device on your local network. Choose Allow. If you chose Block, re-enable Local network for this site under the lock icon, in Site settings. Where the prompt exists, it is also what lets an HTTPS site reach an HTTP device on a private address. Other browsers don’t ask, and keep blocking mixed content.

Browser support

Section titled “Browser support”

Hardware EQ needs a Chromium-based browser — Chrome, Edge or Opera. Firefox and Safari implement none of WebHID, Web Serial or Web Bluetooth, so the panel shows a compatibility notice there instead of the connect buttons. Link with uniPEQ still works there.

Operators

Section titled “Operators”

The device database is read from the eqcaps /v1/ channel on GitHub Pages. EQUALIZER.EQCAPS_URL points it at a mirror or a self-hosted copy — see Customizing the Page. If the database can’t be reached, Hardware EQ still connects devices from known makers, on their protocol’s own limits.
